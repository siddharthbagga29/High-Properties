# Perspective Engine: Neurodivergent Co-Design Charter (v0.1 draft, 2026-10-06)

Owner: Milton (ethics agent). Status: draft for founder review, then for the first paid advisors, who may amend it before it takes effect. Nothing here has been sent to, or agreed with, any real person.

## Summary (read this first)

Perspective Engine builds nothing about neurodivergent (ND) people without paid ND advisors. Advisors hold a binding veto over all simulation content, debrief material, research instruments and public claims; founders and staff cannot override it. Every session ends with a mandatory debrief covering strengths, variability between people, and accommodations, because disability simulations can raise pity and discomfort without improving interaction attitudes (Nario-Redmond 2017). A harm review gates every release, with named stop rules. Section 7 lists claims we will never make, including that the product lets anyone "know what ADHD is like", any diagnostic or treatment claim, and the unsupported figures in the original memo. Recruiting, contracts, payment, IRB submission and any contact with real people are founder-cleared gates (section 9).

## 1. Principles

1. Nothing about us without us. ND people shape, review and can stop what is made about them.
2. Paid, not volunteered. Expertise is compensated; goodwill is not a business model.
3. A simulation is an analogue, not an experience transfer. It can illustrate a few reported experiences; it cannot convey what ADHD is, and group findings do not describe any individual (dossier s4).
4. Strengths, adaptation and variability are required content, not extras (dossier s1: short simulations ignore long-term coping and so understate capability).
5. Reciprocity, not one-way deficit. Mismatch runs in both directions; but the double empathy problem is autism-specific, so its use for ADHD is an extrapolation we label as such (dossier s3).
6. Ethics holds a veto on the product's claims; it does not write marketing copy.

## 2. Advisory board

**Composition (proposal, for founder and advisors to revise).**
- Target roughly 5-7 advisors (a proposal; not derived from evidence). Must include people with ADHD as the largest group, since ADHD is the product focus, plus autistic advisors and advisors with co-occurring conditions (dossier s3 supports autistic input on reciprocity design; it does not make autism and ADHD interchangeable).
- Spread of age, gender, ethnicity, work sector and support needs. ADHD is heterogeneous; no advisor speaks for all.
- Include at least one person who is sceptical of simulations in principle. Their dissent is a feature.
- At least one advisor with working experience of the buyer side (employee or manager) and one researcher or clinician who is themselves ND, if recruitable. Those are additional seats, not substitutes for lived-experience seats.
- Self-identification is sufficient. No diagnosis documents requested or stored.

**Pay and terms.**
- Paid for all time: reviewing, meetings, async comments, and emotional labour of difficult material. Rate to be set by the founder at a professional consulting level; figure unverified, not set here. Pay is not contingent on approval, endorsement or tenure outcomes.
- Written agreement before any work (founder-gated). Plain language, with an accessible format on request.
- Advisors may choose attribution (named, pseudonymous, anonymous) and may withdraw their name or words at any time; withdrawal does not forfeit pay already earned.
- Compensation for sensory-friendly or async participation formats; meetings must offer agendas in advance, cameras optional, written follow-up, and breaks.
- Advisors are independent contractors or equivalent; they do not receive equity or revenue share that would give them a financial reason to approve (a rule that protects the integrity of the veto; the founder may offer separate, non-conditional recognition).

**Role of the board.** Set the content standard, review every release candidate, co-author the debrief, review all external claims, and approve changes to this charter.

## 3. Veto power

**Scope.** Binding over: simulation scenarios, scripts, visuals, audio, interaction mechanics and sensory intensity; debrief content; research instruments (surveys, consent text); recruitment and marketing claims; use of any advisor's words, voice or likeness; any new use of collected data.

**Mechanics (proposal).**
- Any single advisor may place a **hold** on an item. A held item is not shipped, piloted or published.
- A hold is lifted only by (a) a change the holding advisor accepts, or (b) a vote of a majority of advisors, not including founders or staff, after the holder has been heard. Founders and staff have no vote and no power to lift a hold.
- Advisors respond to review requests within an agreed window (to be set with them). Silence is not approval: no response means the item waits. Deadlines are the company's problem, not a reason to proceed.
- Every approval, hold and resolution is logged with date, item, version and reasoning, and stored where all advisors can read it.
- Emergency stop: any advisor can pause a live pilot or deployment for an urgent harm concern. Resume requires the process above.
- Advisors may record a public dissent if the company ships something they have not approved (for instance by changing it after approval). Shipping changed content without re-approval breaches this charter.

**Limits we accept.** The veto is over content and claims, not over the company's existence, pricing or strategy. Advisors are not asked to carry company risk. If advisors collectively conclude the product cannot be made acceptably, that is a recorded outcome the founder must weigh seriously; the charter does not force the founder to continue, only to not ship what advisors have blocked.

## 4. Consent

**Advisors.** Informed, ongoing and revocable. Before starting they are told what material they will review (including potentially upsetting depictions), how their input will be used and credited, how their data and words are stored, and that they can leave at any time with pay for work done.

**Learners and study participants (policy for any future pilot; no one has been contacted).**
- Voluntary opt-in. If an employer purchases the product, completion cannot be made a condition of employment, review or promotion, and the employer cannot see individual responses, timings, gaze or chat. Aggregate reporting only, with a minimum group size to be set by advisors and counsel.
- Plain-language consent that states: this is an analogue, it can cause discomfort, you can stop at any point without consequence, and there is a debrief. Content notes appear before each scenario.
- Learners who are themselves ND get a notice that the content may feel inaccurate or upsetting, plus an opt-out and a route to give feedback to advisors.
- Data minimisation. No diagnostic, medical or "do you have ADHD" questions in the product. No inferring or labelling an individual's neurotype. If gaze is used, process on-device, store no video, and treat it as coarse "where on screen" interaction only; typical webcam error of about 2 to 4 degrees (dossier s10) means it cannot support fixation-level or clinical claims.
- Research use of any data needs separate, explicit consent and IRB or equivalent review (section 8). Jurisdiction-specific privacy duties: unverified, refer to counsel.

## 5. Harm review

**Known harm classes (from the evidence, plus design foresight).**

| Harm | Basis | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Pity, discomfort, helplessness, confusion, embarrassment | Nario-Redmond 2017 (dossier s1) | Debrief; short, bounded scenarios; strengths and adaptation shown; no "fail" states that depict ND people as incompetent |
| Worse interaction attitudes despite higher concern | Same | Measure interaction attitudes and behaviour, not only empathy; stop rule below |
| Underestimating capability | Same (short simulations ignore coping) | Required adaptation, accommodation and strength content |
| Essentialising, "this is what ADHD is" | Dossier s4 (group findings are not individual; heterogeneity) | Scenario labels name the experience analogue, source advisor accounts, and say others differ |
| Manager armchair-diagnosis or surveillance misuse | Foreseeable | Debrief says "do not diagnose or label colleagues"; no individual-level reporting to employers |
| False sense of expertise ("I've done the simulation, I know ADHD") | Foreseeable; emotional-not-cognitive empathy gains (dossier s2) | Debrief language on limits; no certificate wording implying expertise |
| Distress to ND learners | Foreseeable | Content notes, opt-out, sensory controls, feedback route |
| Advisor harm: tokenism, unpaid emotional labour, exposure | Foreseeable | Section 2 pay and attribution rules; check-ins; right to withdraw |
| Misuse of unvalidated science | Dossier s4 | No invented metrics; every scientific statement carries source and caveat |

**Pre-release harm review (every build).** A written checklist covering: advisor approval log complete; each scenario traced to advisor-sourced accounts; debrief attached and unskippable; sensory settings and exits tested; claims register reviewed; data flows reviewed; incident channel live. A release without all items ticked and signed off by the ethics agent and the advisory board does not ship.

**Hard design rules.**
- No "walk a mile in my shoes", "step inside", "feel what it's like" framing.
- Always-available pause, stop and skip. No dark patterns to keep users inside a distressing scene.
- Sensory intensity (audio overload, motion, flashing) capped at levels advisors set, with a reduced option by default and any intense mode opt-in.
- Two-way scenarios where ND and non-ND communication styles each meet a mismatch and both sides can adjust, framed as an interaction problem, not a one-sided deficit.
- No scores that rank, diagnose or grade a person's neurotype; no "attention test".
- Characters are not played for laughs, pity or inspiration.

**Stop rules (proposal; thresholds set with advisors and the IRB).** Pause the pilot or release and convene advisors if: any advisor raises an urgent concern; participants show higher discomfort or worse interaction attitudes after the simulation than before, beyond a pre-registered threshold; any report of a participant being labelled, disciplined or singled out because of the product; a participant in acute distress; a data incident; or the product is described publicly in a way that breaches section 7.

**Incident channel.** A named human contact, monitored, with acknowledgement and review windows to be fixed in writing (founder-gated; targets unverified). ND learners and advisors can raise a concern anonymously. Incidents are logged and reviewed by advisors, not only by staff.

## 6. Debrief (mandatory for every session)

The debrief cannot be skipped or hidden, and completion of the session is not recorded without it. Offered as text, audio and brief video; the reading level and length are set by advisors. It is advisor-authored and approved.

**Required content.**
1. What you just experienced is an analogue built from a few reported experiences. It is not what ADHD is, and it is not what any person's day is like.
2. Strengths and adaptation: how people build workable ways of working; capability, not just difficulty (counters the underestimation finding).
3. Variability: people with ADHD differ widely, and group-level findings are not a description of any one person (dossier s4).
4. Accommodations and practice: concrete, behaviour-level steps (ask what helps, offer written follow-up, agree meeting structure, do not assume), written with advisors.
5. Reciprocity: communication mismatches go both ways. State plainly that the double empathy research comes mainly from autism and is applied here cautiously.
6. Your reaction: pity, discomfort or confusion are common, and they are not a measure of understanding. Pity is not the goal.
7. What not to do next: do not diagnose colleagues, do not say "now I know how you feel", do not repurpose scenes to tease or test anyone.
8. Lived-experience voice: advisor words or recordings, only with consent and credited as the advisor chooses.
9. Where to learn more and how to send feedback or report a concern. Resource list: unverified, to be sourced and vetted by advisors.
10. A check-in prompt for anyone who feels unsettled, with a way to talk to a person.

**Follow-up.** A refresher touchpoint is part of the design. The caregiver-VR literature notes refreshers are likely needed for lasting effect (dossier s1) and the empathy-durability results are mixed (dossier s2); any pilot should include a delayed follow-up (the dossier suggests 4-8 weeks) measuring attitudes and behaviour. We do not claim durability before that data exists.

## 7. Claims we will never make

Prohibited in the product, sales material, decks, investor material, press and by any agent or employee. Anything near these lines goes to the ethics agent and advisors before use.

**About the experience**
- That the product "lets you know what ADHD is like", "walk in their shoes", "feel what it's like", "experience ADHD", or that a simulation transmits the lived experience of any condition.
- That it reproduces any individual's or group's cognition ("neurotypical filtering", "ADHD brain" depicted as measured fact).
- Speaking for all ND or ADHD people, or presenting one advisor's account as typical.

**About science**
- Any "SNR 18 dB" or other invented numeric parameter for attention, filtering or cognition (dossier s4: not an established metric).
- Quantum computing, "zero-latency multi-agent cognition modelling", or any technology claim the build does not use (dossier verdict).
- The 72% success figure, $14.8B TAM, 84% margin or $25k-60k pricing as established facts in external material (dossier verdict: unverified).
- That the product builds empathy, reduces stigma, or improves attitudes, inclusion, retention, productivity or interaction behaviour, unless our own pre-registered results show it, stated with their actual size and limits. The existing evidence is mixed (dossier s1, s2).
- That VR or simulation builds cognitive empathy (dossier s2: d = 0.08 in one meta-analysis) or that gains are "durable" before we have follow-up data.
- That the double empathy problem is an established account of ADHD (it is autism-specific; dossier s3).
- Borrowed vendor statistics (for example "48% faster, 92% more productive", ">90% retention") presented as ours or as fact (dossier s5).
- Prevalence stated without citation and date (the dossier figures are 2.58% persistent and 6.76% symptomatic, Song 2021, and are headline numbers to re-check).

**About health and regulation**
- That the product diagnoses, screens, treats, mitigates or reduces symptoms of ADHD or any condition, or is "clinical", "therapeutic", "clinically validated" or "evidence-based therapy". Scope stays educational or general wellness; counsel to confirm (dossier s8).
- That eye or gaze tracking detects attention disorders or measures attention clinically (dossier s10).
- "FDA-cleared", "FDA-approved", or implied reimbursement, including CPT 0770T eligibility (dossier s8: it is an add-on for VR-assisted therapy, not for training).

**About endorsement and people**
- That ND people or the ND community "endorse", "co-created" or "approved" the product, beyond a statement that names who reviewed what, with their consent and with the veto record behind it.
- Using any advisor's name, quote or likeness without current consent.
- Naming a customer, university, funder or charity as a partner or endorser without written permission.
- Romanticising or reducing: describing ADHD only as a "superpower" or only as a deficit. This is a default, which advisors may refine.

**Process.** Maintain a claims register: each external claim, its source, its caveat, its advisor sign-off, its date. Claims from the dossier are snippet-level and flagged for re-checking against the papers before any quotation. A claim not in the register is not used.

## 8. Research ethics and the IRB packet

This charter is an input to the IRB packet; it is not the packet. Needed, not yet written: study protocol and pre-registration (small pilot with delayed follow-up); consent and assent materials; debrief; harm and stop rules (sections 5, 6); data management plan; advisor review record; recruitment text; and a conflict-of-interest statement covering the founder's commercial interest. ## 9. Gated actions (founder clears; Milton does not perform them)

- Recruiting or contacting any advisor or participant.
- Signing agreements and making any payment.
- Setting pay rates, review windows and incident-response targets.
- Submitting to an IRB or ethics committee, or approaching a research partner.
- Any public statement, press, investor or marketing use of this charter's content.
- Legal review: ADA and employment-law exposure, privacy law, and FDA/general-wellness boundary (counsel).

## 10. Governance

- Charter changes need majority approval of advisors; founders cannot amend alone. Versions are dated and kept; the advisory log is open to advisors.
- Annual review, and a review after any incident or hold dispute.
- Conflicts: the founder and staff declare financial interests. Advisors declare any competing commercial interest.
- Advisors may see any claims register, harm review and incident log entry that concerns their approvals.
- This draft has not been reviewed by any ND person. Until the first advisors approve a version, every statement in it is the ethics agent's proposal.
