01 / The session

One short session, then a check-in 30 days later.

It runs in a browser. No headset is needed.

  1. Step 1 / In session

    Experience

    A short two-way scenario that approximates some reported attentional experiences, co-designed with ADHD adults. Each side of the conversation meets a mismatch and can adjust. It is an analogue, labeled as one. Characters are not played for pity, laughs or inspiration.

  2. Step 2 / In session

    Lived-experience voices

    ADHD advisors describe, in their own words, what helps and what gets in the way. Their words appear only with their consent, credited as they choose. No one voice is presented as typical.

  3. Step 3 / In session

    Accommodation practice

    You rehearse the conversation: ask what helps, offer written follow-up, agree how meetings run, avoid assumptions and labels. A debrief that cannot be skipped covers strengths, how much people differ, and the limits of what you just saw.

  4. Step 4 / Day 30

    30-day follow-up

    A short check-in and refresher a month later. It asks about what you did in real conversations since, not how the session felt. Your organization sees aggregate results only, never individual answers.

02 / What we measure

We measure what managers do, not how they feel.

Strong feelings right after a session do not show that anything changed at work. The pilot's main outcome is behavior at 30 days.

We count

  • Whether managers ask what helps instead of assuming.
  • Whether written follow-up is offered after meetings.
  • Whether meeting structure is agreed with the person.
  • Whether managers avoid diagnosing or labeling colleagues.
  • Interaction attitudes and discomfort, before and after, so we can see if a session makes things worse.

We do not count

  • Satisfaction scores, or whether the session was moving.
  • Empathy as a headline result.
  • Any score that ranks, diagnoses or grades a person's neurotype.
  • Individual responses reported to an employer.

These are the behaviors we intend to measure. Final instruments are drafted with advisors, who can veto them.

We will publish the results, including nulls. If the pilot shows no change, or a change in the wrong direction, we publish that, with sample size and limits.

We write down the measures and the stop thresholds before anyone takes part. If discomfort or attitudes get worse beyond the threshold, we pause.

03 / Co-design

Co-designed with paid ADHD advisors who can say no.

Nothing about ADHD in this product ships without advisors. Our co-design charter commits us to the following.

  • Pay. Advisors are paid for all their time, including the emotional labor of difficult material, whether or not they approve anything.
  • Veto. Any single advisor can place a hold on scenarios, scripts, the debrief, research instruments and public claims. Founders and staff cannot lift a hold. Silence is not approval.
  • Record. Every approval, hold and resolution is logged where all advisors can read it. If we ship something changed after approval, advisors may publish their dissent.
  • Credit. Advisors choose whether to be named, pseudonymous or anonymous, and can withdraw their words at any time.
  • Range. ADHD is varied and no advisor speaks for everyone. The board is meant to include someone skeptical of this kind of tool.

Where this stands, October 2026. The charter is a draft. We have not yet recruited advisors, and no ADHD adult has reviewed it. The first advisors can amend it before it takes effect. Until they approve the content, this page does not claim that anyone endorses the product.

04 / Limits

What we never claim.

  • That anyone can know what ADHD is like, or that the module reproduces any person's experience. There is no certificate implying you now understand ADHD.
  • That it diagnoses, screens, treats or reduces symptoms of anything. It is an educational practice tool, with no attention test and no score on anyone's neurotype.
  • That it builds empathy, reduces stigma, or improves retention, productivity or manager behavior. We say so only if our own results show it, with their size and limits.
  • That it makes an employer compliant with disability law or lowers legal risk. It is practice for conversations, not legal advice.
  • That the ADHD community endorses it, or that one advisor's account is typical.
  • Customer names, quotes, statistics or prices we cannot source and have not had approved. This page has none.
