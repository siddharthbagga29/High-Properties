A short, honest illustration of four attention and timing patterns

You will do one small workplace task twice, in about 15 minutes in all. The second time, four patterns that research reports for some people with ADHD at group level are added in an adjustable, made-up way. Then comes a debrief, a few questions and a planning step.

What this is

  • An interactive illustration of documented attention and timing patterns that some people with ADHD report or show at group level.
  • A way to start a conversation about working practices, always followed by a debrief.

What this is not

  • It is an analogue. It is not what ADHD is, and not what any person's day is like. Group findings do not describe individuals, and people with ADHD differ widely.
  • It does not diagnose, screen, score or rank you or anyone else. It is not an attention test.
  • It has not been shown to build empathy or change behaviour. One review found simulations can raise discomfort without improving attitudes (Nario-Redmond et al., 2017), so this one is short and has a mandatory debrief.

Your data and your control

  • Everything stays on this device. No account, camera, microphone or name. Nothing is sent anywhere. At the end you may download a file that holds no personal identifiers; sharing it is your choice.
  • Stop is visible at every moment. Pause, Ease off and Skip are always available. You can leave at any point without consequence. Everything works from the keyboard; Esc pauses, and the pause dialog has its own Stop button.
  • Content note: interruptions, a wait, notifications from outside the task and, only if you switch sound on, quiet sounds. Nothing flashes. If you have ADHD yourself this may feel inaccurate or upsetting, and you are free to skip it.

Sensory settings (reduced by default)

For facilitators

Open the Facilitator panel (top right) to tune the four conditions before round 2. Defaults are the mechanism spec's mid-range values (intensity 0.5); the session ceiling is 0.8. Hyperfocus (M5) is off and sits under Advanced.

Round 1: the task, as usual

A colleague will send you an email. It is read out and shown phrase by phrase, like a walkthrough on a shared screen. Then:

  1. Wait for Finance's cost summary to open.
  2. Answer 8 short questions about the email.
  3. Follow the instruction at the end of the email.

There is no time limit and no score is shown while you work. Round 2 repeats the same kind of task, with matching content, so you are not just remembering answers. Stop is always at the top right.

Inner voice (off-task thought)

Paused

Everything is frozen, including timers and sound. Nothing is recorded as a response while paused.
