### Tier 1 - Minimum (no entity yet / sole proprietorship): No entity yet: work under your own name (sole proprietorship by default)

No state filing. You keep doing open work in your own name: buyer interviews, drafts, the free USPTO/WIPO name search (entity-checklist step 2), and picking counsel. This is a holding pattern, not a launch vehicle. The repo has no fee figures for this option.

**First-year cost.** $0 in state formation fees (assumption: a sole proprietorship has no state formation filing; a local trade-name filing may apply, but the repo has no fee for it). Name search: free (founder-decisions #6; entity-checklist step 2). EIN: free from the IRS if you want one (entity-checklist step 12). Counsel: $0 at this stage (assumption: nothing is drafted yet).

**For**
- Costs nothing and keeps every option open while the ownership split is decided.
- Avoids paying twice. Amending a Delaware corporation's filing later costs $214 [S] (entity-checklist §4 step 7). Deciding ownership before you file saves that.
- Fits your plan to wait on the university IRB. sbir-draft A18 says the IRB, advisors and site agreements must exist before the estimated 2027-06-01 start of a NIDILRR award. The IRB is not a formation step.

**Against**
- Gmail outreach stays blocked by the repo's own rules. A legal entity (M04) and a real postal address must exist before any batch is sent (gtm/outreach.md Part A postal_address row; Part D rule 6; outreach-log.md checklist item 5).
- No LOIs or paid pilots. The LOI must not be issued before the entity exists or signer authority is clear (gtm/loi-template.md, Part C blanks table). S01 'Close 3 paid pilots' depends on M04 in graph/graph.json.
- No SBIR. In sbir-draft §6, right after the ownership decision (G0), the list needs a 'US for-profit legal entity, plus an EIN' (G1). Whether a sole proprietorship could qualify is not covered in the repo. Ask counsel.
- No company to name as the privacy controller. The repo says the entity is needed before receiving any file or adding a server (entity-checklist §3, privacy map column; §4 step 19).
- You cannot issue shares, SAFEs or options (general knowledge, not from the repo), so no seed raise. In graph.json, S04 waits on S02, which waits on S01, which waits on M04.
- Advisors should be paid from the company (entity-checklist step 18). That needs a business bank account, which needs formation papers and an EIN (step 14).
- No liability shield. This is general knowledge, not from the repo; counsel to confirm.

**Unlocks.** Nothing that needs a company. S01 waits on M04 in graph.json. M05 does not list M04 as a graph dependency, but its gate (SAM.gov registration) needs the entity (sbir-draft G1). V09 (Gmail send) depends only on V08 in the graph, but outreach.md Part D rule 6 requires the entity and a postal address before sending. S04 waits on S02, which waits on S01. One owner (you). It is formed in your home state, so there is no second-state registration. Control and ownership are written in an operating agreement. The repo calls an LLC fine for grants, bootstrapping and angels, and weaker for venture capital (entity-checklist §3 row B).

The repo has no formation-counsel quote, so this reuses the low-to-mode Tier A range of the repo's legal placeholder (sbir-draft §5.4 A9). EIN: free (step 12).

**For**

- It is a for-profit form, so it can meet the SBIR test if the ownership and control test is met (entity-checklist §3 row B).
- It meets the repo's entity rules for outreach, LOIs, pilots, the bank account and the privacy controller.
- Fine for grants, bootstrapping and angel money (entity-checklist §3 row B).

**Against**

### Tier 3 - Standard fundable (Delaware C-corp, founder is sole owner): Delaware C-corp with you holding 100% of the founder stock

The usual investor vehicle. You hold all founder shares under a purchase agreement with vesting. You assign prior Perspective Engine work to the company (Template A). If the shares vest, you file an 83(b) election within 30 days. You can add a co-founder or option pool later, which moves you to Tier 4.

**First-year cost.** State: $109 formation [S] + $175 minimum franchise tax [S] + $50 annual report [S] = $334 in the first 12 months. The tax and report are due March 1 (entity-checklist §2). If you form in 2026, the first payment would fall on 2027-03-01 (my reading, assumption). Optional extras: name reservation $75 for 120 days; next-day expedite $50-100, same-day $100-200 [S] (entity-checklist §2, §4 step 2). Registered agent: required (step 5), but the repo has no price ([U]). Assumption: $100-$300 a year, a placeholder for typical commercial agent fees. If you live outside Delaware, you also register in your home state ('foreign qualification', step 13). That fee and state tax are not in the repo ([U]). Counsel: assumption $1,500-$6,000, reusing the repo's Tier A legal placeholder range (sbir-draft §5.4 A9), since no formation quote exists. Rough total: $434-$634 for state and agent, plus home-state registration, plus counsel. Watch out: the $175 minimum applies only under the authorized-shares method up to 5,000 shares (entity-checklist §2). The repo does not give the tax for more shares, so ask counsel before setting the share count. Late Delaware filing costs a $200 penalty plus 1.5% monthly interest [S] (step 22).

**For**
- The usual vehicle for SAFEs, priced rounds and option pools with US investors [U, market practice] (entity-checklist §3 row A).
- It is the form QSBS (IRC 1202) can apply to; details [U] (entity-checklist §3 row A).
- Simplest SBIR story if you are a US citizen or permanent resident: you own 100% and control the board, well over the 50% line.
- State fees are low ($334). Formation takes an average of 10-15 business days [S] (entity-checklist §2).
- Domestic companies are exempt from the federal BOI report (final rule effective 2026-08-14) [S]; counsel to confirm (entity-checklist §2; step 16).

**Against**
- SBIR is closed if you are not a US citizen or permanent resident. A sole owner who does not qualify fails the more-than-50% test in 13 CFR 121.702 (entity-checklist §1, §3 row A).
- For NIH, the PI's primary employment (more than 50%) must be with the company (sbir-draft R10). NIDILRR wants the principal researcher employed by the business (R3). The founder's current employment is unverified in the repo (sbir-draft 5.5).
- Some steps need an SSN or ITIN: the 83(b) filing and the online EIN form (steps 10, 12). If the responsible party has neither, the EIN comes by phone, fax or mail [S] (step 12). If 25% or more is foreign-owned, extra tax filings such as Form 5472 may apply [U] (step 17).
- Delaware filing slows to 3-4 weeks in March, June and December (entity-checklist §2).
- You pay in two states if you live outside Delaware (step 13).
- The 83(b) deadline is a firm 30 days. Giving it to the company is not filing it (step 10).

**Unlocks.** M04 (cleared once formed). M05 registrations (SAM.gov/UEI, SBA registry, Grants.gov, eRA Commons, Research.gov) can start, but are worth it only if you are a US citizen or permanent resident. Plan for '6 weeks or more' of registrations (sbir-draft R17, §6). S01 paid pilots and LOIs. The Gmail send (V09) entity rule. Privacy controller. S04 seed: this is the right vehicle, though in graph.json S04 formally waits on S02 (pilot results) and V10, not on M04.

### Tier 4 - Most effective (Delaware C-corp with co-founder and/or option pool, US-majority cap table): Delaware C-corp with a co-founder and/or option pool, built so US citizens and permanent residents keep more than 50% and control

Same corporation as Tier 3. It adds founder and co-founder agreements (roles, vesting, control, departures; step 15), an option pool for hires, and board and voting terms. These are written so that US citizens and permanent residents together directly own more than 50% and control the company on every SBIR award date. Advisors get no equity (co-design charter s2; advisory-board.md s1; Template B clause X.1), which also protects the cap table.

**First-year cost.** State: the same $334 as Tier 3 ($109 + $175 + $50; entity-checklist §2). Registered agent: assumption $100-$300 a year, as in Tier 3. Home-state registration if you live outside Delaware: not in the repo ([U]). Counsel: assumption $3,000-$10,000, because there are more documents (co-founder agreement, vesting, control terms, option plan). This reuses the repo's Tier B legal placeholder range (sbir-draft §5.4 A9), since no formation quote exists. Each founder whose shares vest files an 83(b) within 30 days (step 10). The same franchise-tax share-count warning as Tier 3 applies, and it matters more here because an option pool means more authorized shares.

**For**
- Best fit for a seed raise, hiring and pilots. You can show investors a team and a pool.
- It keeps SBIR and STTR open while you add a co-founder and later investors, as long as the majority-and-control rule holds.
- If you are not a US citizen or permanent resident, this is the repo's SBIR route: D01 option A, a US co-founder holding the majority and control (docs/founder-decisions.md #1; entity-checklist §3 row A).
- STTR becomes possible with a formal research-institution partner [U] (entity-checklist §3 row A). That fits the university partner you are already planning.

**Against**
- SBIR test (13 CFR 121.702 as the repo cites it). The company must be for-profit, have a US place of business, and be more than 50% directly owned and controlled by US citizens or permanent residents. Exactly 50% fails; use 51% as a safe margin (entity-checklist §1). It is tested at each Phase I or II award date, so re-check after every SAFE, priced round or option grant (entity-checklist §3 row A).
- If you are not a US citizen or permanent resident, the US co-founder (or a group of US citizens or permanent residents) must hold the majority and also control the board, votes and vesting. You would give up majority control. This is counsel question 1 (entity-checklist §6).
- Seed investors above 50% would end SBIR eligibility at most agencies. Majority VC ownership is allowed only where an agency has adopted it, and one investor is capped at 50%. Which agencies have adopted it is unverified (entity-checklist §3 row A).
- How unvested stock and an unissued option pool count toward the test is not in the repo. Counsel must confirm.
- At NIH, the new foreign-ties disclosure reaches all owners and covered individuals (sbir-draft R2). NIH asks for governing documents and ID proof, such as a passport or naturalization papers (entity-checklist §3 row A; §4 step 1).
- If 25% or more is foreign-owned, extra tax filings such as Form 5472 may apply [U] (step 17).
- Highest legal cost and most signatures. The co-founder must be found and agreed first, which takes time.

**Unlocks.** Everything in Tier 3: M04, M05 registrations, S01 paid pilots, the Gmail send entity rule, and the privacy controller. M05 is also worth pursuing when the founder alone would not qualify, provided the US-majority-and-control structure is real. Plus the strongest S04 seed story (team, pool, a cap table investors expect), and an STTR route with the university partner [U].

### Eligibility rules that decide the choice
- For-profit company with a place of business in the US. A company formed in India or the UK fails on its face. A US subsidiary owned by one also fails on its face, counsel to confirm (entity-checklist.md Summary, §3 row C).
- More than 50% directly owned AND controlled by US citizens or permanent residents (or by US small businesses owned that way), under 13 CFR 121.702. Exactly 50% fails. grants.md and founder-decisions.md say 'at least 51%', which is older wording; 51% is the safe margin either way (entity-checklist.md §1; sbir-draft.md R3).
- Control counts, not just shares: board seats, votes and vesting in a corporation; manager vs member control in an LLC operating agreement (entity-checklist.md §3 rows A and B).
- Ownership is tested at each Phase I or Phase II award date, so every SAFE, priced round or option grant must be re-checked (entity-checklist.md §3 row A).
- Majority VC ownership is allowed only at agencies that have adopted it, and one investor is capped at 50%. Which agencies have adopted it is unverified (entity-checklist.md §3 row A; sbir-draft.md R3).
- Fewer than 500 employees (sbir-draft.md R3).
- NIH: the PI's primary employment (more than 50%) must be with the company, and normally at least 67% of Phase I work is done by the company (R10). NIDILRR: at least two-thirds of research or analytic activity by the company (R6). NSF: at least two-thirds of the budget to the company (R20).
- NIDILRR: foreign entities are not eligible. The company must be American-owned, independent and for-profit, with the principal researcher employed by the business (sbir-draft.md R3).
- Registrations come after formation and EIN: Login.gov, SAM.gov (UEI), SBA Company Registry, Grants.gov, eRA Commons (NIH), Research.gov (NSF). Allow '6 weeks or more' for the full set. The SAM Entity Administrator must be an employee, officer or board member [V2] (sbir-draft.md R17, §6 G1-G8).
- Proof is required: NIH asks for governing documents and ID proof such as a passport or naturalization papers (entity-checklist.md §3 row A, §4 step 1).
- At NIH, the new foreign-ties disclosure covers all owners and covered individuals (sbir-draft.md R2).
- STTR also needs a formal research-institution partner [U] (entity-checklist.md §3 row A).
- Advisors get no equity (co-design charter s2; Template B clause X.1), which keeps the cap table clean for the majority test (entity-checklist.md §1, §5).
- Not covered in the repo, so counsel must confirm: whether a sole proprietorship can qualify, and how unvested stock or an unissued option pool counts.

### Recommendation
Form a Delaware C-corp. Whether that means Tier 3 or Tier 4 depends on one fact the repo does not record: will US citizens or permanent residents hold more than 50% of the company, and control it, at each award date? (entity-checklist.md §1, §3 row A).

(1) If you are a US citizen or permanent resident: form Tier 3 now, with you as sole owner. Delaware state fees are about $334 in the first 12 months ($109 + $175 + $50; entity-checklist.md §2). Add a registered agent (assumed $100-$300), home-state registration if you live outside Delaware (not priced in the repo), and counsel (assumed $1,500-$6,000). Add a co-founder or option pool later (Tier 4), keeping US-person ownership at 51% or more after each round.

(2) If you are not: SBIR needs Tier 4, with a US co-founder (or a group of US citizens or permanent residents) holding the majority and the control (founder-decisions.md #1 option A). If giving up majority control is not acceptable, still form a Delaware C-corp for pilots and seed. Treat SBIR, STTR and NIDILRR as closed and fund from pilots and equity.

Tier 2 (home-state LLC) fits only if no outside investors are planned (entity-checklist.md §3, working recommendation). That holds whether or not SBIR is in play. The model's peak funding need is P50 $4.93M before grants or financing (model.md, Results, Cash), against Phase I awards of about $95k-$314k, so outside equity is likely. Converting an LLC to a C-corp later adds cost.

Order and timing:
- Run the free name search first. A third-party record shows a suspended PERSPECTIVE ENGINE mark, serial 88900125 [U] (entity-checklist.md §1).
- Settle the ownership split with counsel before filing. A corporate amendment costs $214 [S] (step 7).
- Delaware takes 10-15 business days on average, and 3-4 weeks in March, June and December (§2).
- For the NIDILRR deadline (estimated 2026-12-16), registrations must start by 2026-11-04 with the company and EIN already in place (sbir-draft.md §6). That is 27 days from today, which is tight, and the notice may not post at all (R26). Realistic targets: NIH 2027-01-05 (start registrations by 2026-11-24), NIH 2027-04-05 (start by 2027-02-22), or NSF 2027-03-04 after a Project Pitch (start by 2027-01-21).
- Waiting on the university IRB is fine for formation. It is pre-award work that must exist before the estimated 2027-06-01 NIDILRR start (A18). NIH and NSF start dates are not in the repo.

Forming the company also unblocks:
- Gmail outreach. The repo's send rules need the entity and a postal address (gtm/outreach.md Part D rule 6).
- LOIs and paid pilots (S01 depends on M04).
- Paying advisors from a company account (entity-checklist.md step 18). The advisor budget is about $4.7k-$7k per quarter (founder-decisions.md #2; advisory-board.md §1 gives $4,650 at $50/h and $6,975 at $75/h).

### What each tier buys and gives up
**Minimum.** BUYS: a Delaware C-corp. Under the repo's own rules this lets you send outreach batch one, which needs the entity plus a real postal address (gtm/outreach.md Part D.6). It also lets you pay advisors from the company (entity-checklist s4 step 18). It buys one quarter of a paid advisory board at the $50/h floor. Those are the 'paid advisor sessions'. Every applicant gets a paid 30-minute screening call, selected or not. Each advisor then gets 1.5 h onboarding, 2 h of charter review, a 90-minute Session 1 (2 h with prep), three 60-90 minute content-review sessions on the browser MVP using the advisor-review-kit form, 4 h of async review and a 0.5 h check-in (advisory-board s1, s4). Any one advisor can place a binding hold, and pay never depends on approval. The founder facilitates with a paid note-taker, sends batch one and later batches, and works toward 50 discovery conversations and LOIs. This runs the kill tests that do not need the IRB study: H3 buyer (fewer than 3 LOIs from 50 qualified conversations is the kill signal), H4 delivery (MVP in 5 facilitated sessions), and the advisor-review part of H2 harm (execution-plan s1). GIVES UP: no IRB and no randomized study, so no evidence on H1, the main value question. The OSF pre-registration stays a draft, because posting needs an ethics approval reference (preregistration.md 0.2 item 2). The advisor rate is at the floor, which advisory-board s1 says probably understates fair pay. No paid help. Any paid pilot is run by the founder alone. The month-12 goal is not met.

**Lean.** BUYS: everything in Minimum, plus advisors for 2 quarters at the A8 mode. It adds a university partner that serves as IRB of record and supplies a faculty sponsor, an independent blinded analyst and a safety reviewer. It runs a 40-manager team-randomized feasibility pilot with $25 incentives and pays a part-time study operations lead, a survey platform, and legal work for site agreements. This is the scope of the SBIR Tier A draft. A NIDILRR award would fund similar costs only from its start date (estimated 2027-06-01); work before that is out of pocket (sbir-draft A18). GIVES UP: 40 managers is below the pre-registration minimum of 80. Below 80, the pre-registration says the study is reported as a feasibility description with estimates and CIs but no inference claims (preregistration.md Stopping rule). sbir-draft says that at 40 only feasibility and harm signals are interpretable. Advisors are funded for only 6 months, even though the charter has them review every release candidate (codesign-charter s2). There is no hired facilitator, no external accessibility audit and no pilot facilitator, so the founder runs the paid pilots. The month-12 goal is only partly met.

**Most effective.** BUYS: the fastest credible path to the month-12 goal. It funds 4 quarters of advisors at about $75/h, a hired facilitator (ND-affiliated where possible) and note-taker, and a 120-manager pre-registered pilot (expected 95% CI half-width about 0.41 SD, preregistration.md 0.1) through the university subaward. It pays a part-time engineer and an operations lead, an external accessibility audit, facilitation for 3 paid pilots, legal work for pilot contracts and founder documents, travel and insurance. This matches the NIH/NSF Tier B scope. GIVES UP: it needs about $201k before founder pay. That realistically means an SBIR award or outside money. The only award start date in the repo is NIDILRR's estimated 2027-06-01 (R4), and that is a Tier A award. NIH and NSF start dates are not in the repo (assumption: not earlier). Even this pilot cannot confirm a small effect: d = 0.36 needs 312 managers (preregistration Sample size rationale). Advisors, IRB approval and the build freeze all come before enrolment (irb-packet s0 sequence). So delaying the IRB likely pushes the day-30 readout, and the paper, past month 12 (judgment).

### Non-dilutive funding that could offset it
All three SBIR routes share the ownership rule. The company must be a for-profit with a US place of business, more than 50% owned and controlled by US citizens or permanent residents (13 CFR 121.702; legal/entity-checklist.md s1; sbir-draft R3). NIH also needs the PI's primary employment (more than 50%) with the company (R10). NIDILRR wants the principal researcher employed by the business (R3). The NSF rule on this is not in the repo. If the majority owners are not US citizens or permanent residents, none of the SBIR options below apply, unless US citizens or permanent residents (for example a US co-founder) hold the majority and control.

(1) NIDILRR SBIR Phase I (ACL/HHS). $95,000-$100,000 per award, 6 months, 11 awards. It is a forecast only: estimated posting 2026-10-16, estimated due date 2026-12-16 (plan for about 2026-12-11), estimated start 2027-06-01 (sbir-draft R4, s6). It is the best topical fit (fit 0.80, P 0.20, both judgment calls in grants.md). It matches the Lean tier. It may not post at all: a coalition letter reports 36 FY2026 NIDILRR competitions removed (R26). Registrations take 6 weeks or more and must start by 2026-11-04, with the entity and EIN already in place (sbir-draft s6). If you wait on incorporating, this cycle is out of reach.

(2) NIH SBIR Phase I (R43, PA-27-100). The cap is $314,363 per sbir-draft R9 (grants.md's $306,872 is older; the FY2027 value is unverified, and one third-party guide lists $323,090). Receipt dates are 2027-01-05 (start registrations by 2026-11-24) and 2027-04-05 (start by 2027-02-22). It needs a health-relevant outcome and a program-officer call (grants.md row 1; sbir-draft s4). It fits Most effective (Tier B request $269,441).

(3) NSF SBIR Phase I (NSF 26-510). Up to $305,000 over 6-18 months. A Project Pitch comes first (rolling, at most 2 per company per year). Full proposals are due 2027-03-04 (start registrations by 2027-01-21) or 2027-07-07 (R18, s6). It also fits Tier B.

(4) NIH Small Business Transition Grant (PAR-27-039). Up to $400k for Phase I, expected to post 2026-11, due 2027-01-05. It applies only to an early-career scientist founder, and that eligibility is unverified (grants.md row 4).

Budget rules: advisors, legal and the university subaward together must stay at or under one-third of the request (sbir-draft 5.1, 5.3; R6, R10, R20). The subaward is the swing line (5.3). The fee is 7% at NIH and NSF; NIDILRR treatment is unverified (A13). The indirect-cost rule is unresolved (R12). Award probabilities are assumptions (0.20 / 0.12 / 0.10), so do not plan cash flow on them. Work before an award starts (advisors, IRB route, site letters) is paid out of pocket (sbir-draft A18).

Other routes: the Inclusive Innovators Pitch Competition in Canada (events Vancouver 2026-10-19, Calgary 2026-11-10) is cheap to check, but the prize amount, application deadline and non-Canadian eligibility are unverified (grants.md s3). Not viable: Kessler (grantmaking ends by 2026-12-31; final cycle restricted to AL, AR, KY, MS and WV), DIF x Tech (excludes for-profits and LLCs) and IES SBIR (education scope).

Not a grant, but it also offsets costs: revenue from paid pilots. finance/model.md assumes $4k / $12k / $30k per pilot, so 3 pilots bring about $36k at the mode. The model gives a 68% chance of 3 or more paid pilots signed in 2027. All of these figures are assumptions.

### Recommendation
Choose Lean, but get there in stages from Minimum.

Start Minimum now, at about $8.2k (about $9.7k if counsel quotes the A9 Tier A mode). It runs the kill tests that do not need the IRB study before you spend on one: buyers (batch one and LOIs), delivery (5 facilitated sessions) and the advisor-review part of harm.

One caution about waiting to incorporate. You can delay the IRB at no cost. Delaying incorporation is different. At $784 (only $334 of it is sourced Delaware state fees; the rest is assumed agent and home-state fees), it is a small line, and four other steps depend on it:
- Under gtm/outreach.md Part D.6, batch one cannot be sent until the legal entity and a real postal address exist.
- Advisors are paid from the company (entity-checklist step 18).
- A form backend or server needs a named controller first (privacy.md s9), and the repo says that controller is the company (entity-checklist s3, step 19).
- Every SBIR registration needs the entity and EIN (sbir-draft G1).

Move to Lean, at about $41.7k, once three things are true: advisors have approved a build, you have LOIs, and the university partner is ready to submit to the IRB. Lean has the same scope as the NIDILRR Tier A draft. An award would fund that scope only from its start date (estimated 2027-06-01); anything spent before then stays out of pocket (sbir-draft A18).

Treat Most effective, at about $201k, as the budget for an SBIR Tier B application, not as cash to spend first. That means NIH on 2027-04-05 (incorporate early enough to start registrations by 2027-02-22) or NSF on 2027-03-04 (start by 2027-01-21) or 2027-07-07.

To be plain about it: with incorporation and the IRB deferred, the month-12 target as written is unlikely. That target is a pre-registered 80-150 manager estimation pilot, 3 paid enterprise pilots and one non-dilutive grant submitted (docs/execution-plan.md summary). Lean realistically delivers a feasibility pilot, LOIs and possibly paid pilots by then. The 120-manager study moves to year 2, funded by an SBIR if the ownership test is met.
